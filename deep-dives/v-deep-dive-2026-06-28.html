🎯 MOSE Verdict

Visa is as close to a perfect business as exists: it owns half of a global payments duopoly (with Mastercard), takes a tiny toll on an ever-rising tide of electronic transactions, carries almost no credit risk, and converts ~60% of revenue to free cash flow. The quality is why Chris Hohn (TCI) has made it a 20% position and Chuck Akre, Terry Smith, Dev Kantesaria and Seth Klarman all hold it.

Action: core long-term hold; accumulate on weakness. At ~$341 it trades ~29% above our base owner-earnings value of $264 and well above the 25%-MOS price of ~$198 — a quality-at-a-premium situation. There's no margin of safety today; add on pullbacks (a 10–15% dip toward the high-$280s/low-$300s) or simply hold and let it compound.

🏰 Business & Moat

Visa operates the rails — not a lender. It earns fees every time its ~4.5B cards are used across 130M+ merchant locations. The moat is a textbook two-sided network effect: more cardholders attract more merchants and vice-versa, a position essentially impossible to dislodge. Add global scale, brand, and regulatory/security infrastructure, and you have one of the widest moats in the market. Secular tailwind: the multi-decade shift from cash to digital payments still has a long runway, especially internationally and in new flows (B2B, cross-border, real-time payments).

📈 Bull Case

  • Duopoly economics with ~67% operating margins and minimal capital needs.
  • Long cash-to-digital runway plus new flows (Visa Direct, B2B, cross-border).
  • Relentless buybacks + growing dividend; a compounding machine.
  • Inflation is a quiet tailwind — fees scale with transaction value.

📉 Risks

  • No margin of safety at ~32× earnings / 29% above base IV.
  • Regulatory pressure on interchange fees (Durbin, EU caps, litigation).
  • Disruption risk: real-time bank rails, stablecoins, "pay-by-bank."
  • Slowing consumer spending in a downturn dents volume growth.
