🎯 MOSE Verdict

GE Aerospace (the jewel left after the GE breakup) is one of the best industrial businesses in the world: it makes jet engines and — crucially — earns decades of high-margin aftermarket service revenue on every engine in the air. The installed base is a multi-decade annuity. Chris Hohn (TCI) has made it a ~30% position — extraordinary conviction. The tension: the MOSE model sees it ~28% above a $266 intrinsic value at ~46x earnings.

Action: WATCH — wonderful business, demanding price. Hohn is underwriting years of aftermarket compounding and margin expansion that justify the premium; the model says you have no cushion today. Hold if owned; accumulate on an aviation-cycle scare.

🏰 Business & Moat

Commercial aerospace is a duopoly/oligopoly (GE/CFM vs Rolls/Pratt). Engines are sold near cost; the money is in servicing them for 20–30 years — a razor-and-blades model with enormous switching costs and pricing power. Add defense engines and a multi-year aircraft delivery backlog, and GE Aerospace has one of the most durable, visible earnings streams in industrials.

📈 Bull Case

  • Aftermarket annuity on a huge, growing installed base — decades of visibility.
  • Aviation demand super-cycle; margin expansion runway.
  • Oligopoly with massive switching costs and pricing power.
  • TCIs ~30% conviction.

📉 Risks

  • ~46x earnings, ~28% above model IV — priced for execution.
  • Aviation is cyclical; a travel shock hits service volumes.
  • Supply-chain and engine-program execution risk.
  • Single-segment concentration after the breakup.
