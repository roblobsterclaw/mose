🎯 MOSE Verdict

Occidental is a Permian-Basin-focused oil & gas producer with a chemicals arm (OxyChem) and a bet on carbon capture. Its real claim to fame is Warren Buffett, who owns a large stake (~6.6% of Berkshires equity book) plus warrants. The MOSE owner-earnings model — which penalizes a commodity producer with negative recent revenue growth — flags it as expensive (~$33 IV vs $50).

Action: WATCH — own it as a Buffett-aligned energy/inflation hedge, not as a value compounder. Oil producers are price-takers; intrinsic value swings with crude. The bull case is Buffetts implicit floor, Permian quality, and OxyChem cash flow. Best added on energy/oil-price weakness.

🏰 Business & Moat

OXY has high-quality, low-cost Permian acreage, a sizable chemicals business (a real cash generator), and a long-dated option on Direct Air Capture / carbon management. The "moat" in E&P is asset quality and a low cost of production — not a durable competitive moat in the Buffett sense. Earnings are inherently tied to the oil price.

📈 Bull Case

  • Buffetts large, growing stake provides a perceived floor.
  • Top-tier Permian assets + steady OxyChem cash flow.
  • Inflation / energy-shock hedge for the portfolio.
  • Optionality on carbon capture if it scales.

📉 Risks

  • Commodity price-taker — earnings and value swing with crude.
  • Model flags it rich (~$33 IV); negative recent revenue growth.
  • Capital-intensive; debt load from the Anadarko deal.
  • Energy transition is a long-term demand overhang.

🧮 Valuation

The owner-earnings DCF (bear $33 / base $33 / bull $34) is intentionally tight and conservative for a commodity producer — it does not capture an oil-price up-cycle. Treat OXY as a macro/energy allocation anchored by Buffetts conviction rather than a precise intrinsic-value buy. P/E ~37xRev CAGR −5% Accumulate on oil weakness.
